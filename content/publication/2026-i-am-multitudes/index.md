abstract: 'Natural Language Processing (NLP) systems engage with identity across datasets,
  models, tasks, and evaluation frameworks, yet they often do so by reducing people
  to fixed categories (e.g., race, gender, age). This reduction is particularly consequential
  in identity-sensitive applications: for example, it affects how systems classify
  harm or generate tailored responses to user needs. But individuals contain multitudes:
  they are not a homogeneous group, but are situated, context-dependent selves whose
  differences exceed fixed categories. To examine how identity is addressed in NLP,
  we survey ACL Anthology papers that mention "identity". Our study investigates how
  the term is operationalized, where it enters the NLP pipeline, which tasks incorporate
  identity-related information, and whether its use is explicit or implicit. Our analysis
  shows that identity is most often operationalized through explicit sociodemographic
  categories, especially those referring to protected groups by law. Moreover, it
  is primarily used during the evaluation stage to audit systems for bias and harm.
  We synthesize these patterns into a taxonomy of identity operationalizations in
  NLP. Finally, we propose a roadmap to treat identity as relational, contextual,
  intersectional, and linguistically indexed, with the broader scope of achieving
  socially aware NLP.'
